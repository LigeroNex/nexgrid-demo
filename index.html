The problem

Flexibility is easy to promise and hard to prove.

Most sites know their bill. Few can say how much load can really move without breaking a process, or what a shift was worth once it was made.

A forecast is not a decision

Knowing tomorrow's demand does not tell you which load can move, when, or by how much.

The limits live in people's heads

What can safely move depends on temperatures, processes and contracts that are rarely written down in any system.

Savings are argued about afterwards

Without a baseline agreed in advance, a saving is a claim your finance team has no reason to accept.

How it works

From meter data to a measured result, in six steps.

  1. 01

    Check the data

    Half-hourly meter, generation and asset data, checked for units, gaps, duplicates and clock changes before it is used.

  2. 02

    Forecast

    Demand and on-site generation for the day ahead. A forecast that does not beat simple baselines is not used.

  3. 03

    Record the limits

    Physical, contractual and product limits, written down with your site team and kept as versions.

  4. 04

    Find what can move

    An optimiser proposes shifts that stay inside every limit. A plan that breaks one cannot be produced.

  5. 05

    A person decides

    Each opportunity shows the limit that binds it, its confidence and a value range. You approve or reject.

  6. 06

    Measure the result

    What actually happened, against a baseline whose method was agreed before the first recommendation.

How it is designed

Built for the person who has to act on it.

NexGrid is designed around four ideas.

Every limit is a hard limit

The optimiser works inside your limits, so a plan that breaks one cannot be produced. Zero broken limits is a release rule.

Nothing beats a weak recommendation

When data is stale or confidence is low, NexGrid recommends nothing, and says why.

Every number can be reproduced

Each plan records its data snapshot and versions, so it can be recreated exactly, even after the meter data is corrected.

Value against an agreed baseline

How the saving is measured is agreed in writing first, so the result is a measurement and not a claim.

No language model decides anything. Forecasts use statistical methods that are tested against simple baselines, and the schedule comes from a mathematical optimiser. Every recommendation can be traced to one run and one data snapshot.

Limits

What NexGrid does not do.

A product that influences physical equipment has to be clear about where it stops. We put these in writing at the start.

  • No control of assetsNexGrid never switches anything. Every action is taken by an authorised person on your site.
  • No hidden limitsEvery recommendation shows the limit that binds it, its confidence and a value range.
  • No guessing on stale dataIf a feed is out of date, that asset gets no recommendation until it reports again.
  • No tradingNexGrid does not bid, trade or settle, and makes no market or regulatory representation.
  • No household dataBusiness sites only. NexGrid does not read household smart-meter data.
  • People decideApproving a change, and changing a limit, are decisions for named people.
